German Sentence Structure & Word Order

Understand the core architecture of German sentences: the golden V2 rule, position 1 inversion, subordinate clause verb-end rules, and the TeKaMoLo formula.

1. Main Clause: The V2 Rule (Verb Position 2)

Golden Rule: Verb = Position 2

In standard German declarative main clauses, the conjugated verb is ALWAYS in Position 2. Position 1 can be occupied by the Subject, a Time expression, a Location, or an Object. Position 1 counts as ONE grammatical element (not necessarily a single word!).

Position 1 (Subject) Ich
Position 2 (Verb) kaufe
Middle (Object) heute einen Apfel
End (Satzklammer) .

2. Position 1 Inversion (Subject-Verb Swap)

Inversion: Subject moves to Position 3

If any element other than the Subject is placed in Position 1 (such as a time element, location, or prepositional phrase), the verb STAYS in Position 2, and the Subject moves immediately to Position 3.

Position 1 (Time) Heute
Position 2 (Verb) kaufe
Position 3 (Subject) ich
Middle (Object) einen Apfel

3. Subordinate Clause (Nebensatz): Verb-End Rule

Subordinating Conjunction → Verb at End

Subordinating conjunctions (e.g. weil, dass, obwohl, wenn, als, da) kick the conjugated verb to the very end of the clause.

Conjunction ... weil
Subject ich
Object Hunger
Clause End (Verb) habe.

4. TeKaMoLo Rule (Adverbial Order)

Temporal → Kausal → Modal → Lokal
